# Research Record Guide

> Disclaimer: These guidelines will be adjusted according to lab operations, research needs, and differences between research fields

## 1. Let's Be Clear First

Research in this lab may include:

- Artificial intelligence and engineering implementation
- Management research and business analytics
- Social science research and data analysis

Although research methods differ, some basic requirements are the same:

> **Research must be understandable, traceable, reproducible, and transferable.**

The purpose of this system is not to add formal burden, but to avoid the following problems:

- Research that was done leaves no usable record
- Students themselves cannot understand their own work after some time
- When writing a thesis or report, you cannot go back and organize what was done
- After a student graduates, the data, code, or analysis workflow is broken off
- There is no common management standard across different types of research

## 2. Core Principles

### 1. Research that is not recorded is the same as research not done

Research is judged not only by its results, but also by whether the process can be clearly explained.

At minimum, you must be able to answer:

- What did you do?
- Why did you do it this way?
- What data, methods, or tools did you use?
- What results did you get?
- What is the next step?

### 2. Research should be understandable to others

Your records should not be understandable only to you. They should allow:

- Your supervisor
- Whoever takes over in the future
- Yourself a few months from now

to quickly understand the context of the research.

### 3. Research should be traceable

Every research result should be traceable back to:

- The corresponding research question
- The data used
- The method used
- The version used
- The analysis or implementation process

### 4. Research should be continuable

Research is not a one-off attempt, but work that can be built upon.

This means:

- Files cannot be scattered randomly
- Data cannot exist only on a personal computer
- Code, analyses, and documents need a basic structure
- At graduation or handover, the work should be able to continue

## 3. Scope

This system applies to the following types of research:

### A. AI / Engineering Research
For example:

- Machine learning
- Deep learning
- System development
- Model training and evaluation
- Algorithm implementation

### B. Management Research
For example:

- Analysis of management issues
- Business model research
- Case studies
- Questionnaire data analysis
- Organizational or strategy research

### C. Social Science Research
For example:

- Questionnaire surveys
- Interview studies
- Behavioral research
- Secondary data analysis
- Empirical research

## 4. Common Requirements

Regardless of research type, every member must complete the following.

### 1. Create research records

Each research topic should have a continuously updated record.

The record can be in:

- Markdown
- Google Docs
- Notion
- Other digital formats that have been approved

In principle, however, it should meet the following requirements:

- Accessible
- Preservable
- Searchable
- Transferable

### 2. Every research activity must leave a record

For every important research activity, record at least:

- Date
- Person in charge
- Research question or objective
- Method used
- Data or materials used
- Execution process
- Results
- Problems and observations
- Next step

### 3. Record failures, not just successes

Do not record only the results that look good.

The following also need to be recorded:

- Failed attempts
- Methods that did not work
- Hypotheses that did not hold
- Reasons for changes made along the way
- Results that did not match expectations, and the judgment made at the time

These are equally important for later research.

> **Special note:** If a result cannot be reproduced, or the data shows anomalies, record it immediately and inform me, instead of deleting or skipping it on your own. The reliability of research rests on true and complete records, not on selectively presenting results. How a lab treats data that does not match expectations often reflects the research integrity and scientific attitude of that environment.

## 5. Research Data and Document Management

### 1. Do not store only on personal devices

The following are not acceptable:

- Research data exists only on a personal computer
- Code is not synced
- Documents are accessible only through a personal account
- Others cannot take over after you leave the lab

All formal research data should be stored in a space designated by the lab or one that can be shared.

### 2. File names must be clear

Avoid names like the following:

- `test`
- `final`
- `final2`
- `new`
- `最新版`
- `真的最終版`

Recommended names include:

- Date
- Topic
- Version
- Content type

For example:

- `2026-04-16-literature-review.md`
- `survey-analysis-v01.xlsx`
- `model-baseline-exp03.md`

### 3. Folder structure must be consistent

Each research topic should include at least:

- `docs/`: research documents, meeting notes, literature reviews
- `data/`: data
- `analysis/`: analysis process or results
- `code/`: code (if applicable)
- `outputs/`: figures, models, tables, report outputs

## 6. Additional Requirements by Research Type

### A. Additional Requirements for AI / Engineering Research

If the research involves code, models, or systems, you must also:

- Use Git or equivalent version control
- Record the code version
- Record the data version
- Record the main parameter settings
- Make results traceable to a specific version and settings

At minimum, you must be able to answer:

- Which version produced this result?
- Which dataset was used?
- What were the parameters?
- Can it be run again?

### B. Additional Requirements for Management Research

If the research involves cases, business analysis, or management theory, you must also:

- Clearly state the research question
- Clearly record the theoretical or literature basis
- Clearly state the data sources
- Clearly record the analysis logic
- Distinguish between "facts from the data" and "the researcher's inferences"

At minimum, you must be able to answer:

- What is the basis for this conclusion?
- What data was used?
- Why was this analysis method chosen?
- Are there other possible explanations?

### C. Additional Requirements for Social Science Research

If the research involves questionnaires, interviews, participant data, or social data analysis, you must also:

- Clearly record the research design
- Clearly record the sample source and selection criteria
- Clearly record the data collection method
- Clearly record the data cleaning and transformation process
- Comply with research ethics and privacy requirements

At minimum, you must be able to answer:

- How was the data obtained?
- How was the sample selected?
- Were there data cleaning or exclusion criteria?
- Does it involve anonymization and personal data protection?

## 7. Minimum Standard for Research Records

Every research record should include at least the following fields:

1. Basic information  
2. Research question or objective  
3. Method or research design  
4. Data / materials / sample used  
5. Execution process  
6. Results  
7. Problems and observations  
8. Conclusion  
9. Next step

Use the standard template provided by the lab.

## 8. Wrap-Up Before Handover and Graduation

Before the research ends, a student graduates, or a handover takes place, complete the following:

- Summary of the research topic
- Location of the main data
- Location of the main documents
- Organized code or analysis files
- Explanation of research results
- Unfinished items
- Suggested future directions

The minimum standard for handover is:

> **Whoever takes over can understand and continue your research within a reasonable amount of time.**

## 9. Common Misconceptions

### ❌ "It's enough that I know it myself"
→ Research is not only for your present self to read

### ❌ "I'll record it once it works"
→ Failures and revisions are equally important

### ❌ "Do it first, organize it later"
→ In most cases, it never gets fully organized later

### ❌ "Different fields don't need unified management"
→ Research methods differ, but the management logic needs to be consistent

## 10. Key Takeaways

Remember these four things:

1. **No record means the research is not done**
2. **Research must be understandable**
3. **Research must be traceable**
4. **Research must be continuable**

The purpose of this system is not to add formal burden, but to:

> **Keep research from being interrupted when people leave, time passes, or files become disorganized.**
