# Undergraduate Project Team Guide

> This document explains the department's official rules, actual timeline, and submission requirements. The department's official announcements, as issued by the department office, take precedence; this document is only an organized reference.

---

## 1. Course Positioning and Grouping Rules

The graduation project is the IEM department's Capstone (capstone/integrative course), a required 3-credit course in the fall semester of the fourth year. Its aim is to train students to address industrial engineering and management issues through **teamwork**.

### Grouping Rules

- Groups must have **2–3 students** (3 recommended)
- Find your partners on your own first, then seek a faculty advisor as a group
- Each faculty member can advise at most 6 project students, until the quota is filled
- Students who do not submit the forms within the required time will be assigned to groups by the department, and no objections will be accepted

If you need to change advisors after submitting the Advisor Consent Form, both the new and the former advisor must agree. ## 3. Teamwork Structure

The department requires one of the following two structures. Describe it in the Graduation Project Application, and attach a **teamwork structure diagram** (placed on the page before the report's table of contents).

**Parallel:** Under the same topic, each member is responsible for a different sub-topic
- Same problem, different methods
- Or same problem, different scopes

**Sequential:** Each member is responsible for a different stage of the topic
- For example: define the problem → experiment → statistical analysis

## 5. Midterm Report

### Time

Weeks 5–6 of the fourth-year fall semester (around mid-October), all day. There are morning and afternoon sessions, with multiple classrooms running at the same time in each session.

### Submission (one week before the report)

Submit to the teaching assistant (hard copy and electronic files):

- Printed Word report × 2 copies (stapled; no hardcover binding needed)
- Printed PPT × 2 copies (stapled)
- Word and PPT electronic files × 1 each
- Scanned weekly work log form (after your advisor adds comments and signs it, attach it at the end of the Word report and bind it into the hard copy)
- Scanned teamwork structure diagram (placed on the page before the Word report's table of contents)

> When submitting hard copies, mark your group code in the **top right corner** of the Word and PPT (e.g., A11, D34).

### On the Day of the Report

- **15 minutes** per group: 10-minute presentation, 5-minute Q&A
- Each classroom has a multimedia teaching podium
- Load your PPT onto the computer **10 minutes** before your session starts
- **Dress: formal attire (men: suit and dress shirt; women: suit)**

### Review Committee

Department professors and graduate teaching assistants

### Review Focus

- Innovativeness and difficulty of the research problem
- Completeness of the improvement plan and proposal report content
- Q&A skills

## 6. Final Report

### Time

Weeks 15–16 of the fourth-year fall semester (around mid-December), all day, scheduled the same way as the midterm report.

### Submission (one week before the report)

Submit to the teaching assistant (hard copy and electronic files):

- Printed Word report × 3 copies (stapled, including the following attachments)
  1. Scanned teamwork structure diagram (on the page before the table of contents)
  2. Explanation of revisions made in response to midterm review comments (attached after the Word report)
  3. Scanned weekly work log form, with your advisor's midterm and final signatures (last attachment)
- Printed PPT × 3 copies (stapled, with the A4 poster on the last page)
- **A1 poster** × 1
- Electronic files of all of the above

### On the Day of the Report

- **15 minutes** per group: 10-minute presentation, 5-minute Q&A
- **Dress: formal attire (men: suit and dress shirt; women: suit)**
- You must help move the poster stands (gather at the elevator by the department office, at the announced time)

### Review Committee

External representatives (from industry or academia), department professors, graduate teaching assistants

### Grading Criteria

| Item | Weight |
|------|------|
| Value of the project | 30% |
| Overall structure of the project | 30% |
| Written report | 20% |
| Presentation performance | 10% |
| Poster design and content presentation | 10% |

### Review Focus

- Whether the midterm review comments have actually been addressed
- Integration of the final report content with what has been learned
- Completeness of the report content and Q&A skills

### Final Version Submission (after your advisor's approval)

- **Printed Word report**: blue cover with waterproof lamination, with a spine; the original graduation project approval form signed by your advisor is bound inside the cover
- **Printed PPT**: mark the group code in the top right corner, and insert it at the first page of the printed Word report

## 8. Division of Work and Contributions

### Division of Work Principles

- Each team member must have a clear area of responsibility that can be reviewed
- When using the Parallel or Sequential structure, explain the division of work clearly in the application
- "I'm in charge of integration" is not a complete description of a role

### Contribution Records

In the final report, each team member must clearly explain:

- Which parts I was responsible for
- What actual work I did (backed by evidence, such as commits, documents, weekly work log entries)
- What I learned in the process

### Handling Unfair Division of Work

If a team member is clearly not contributing and this affects overall progress:

1. First discuss it within the team and give them a chance to improve
2. If there is no improvement, tell me, and I will step in to discuss it
3. I will reflect this truthfully when signing off on the weekly work log, which will affect that member's eligibility to present and their grade
