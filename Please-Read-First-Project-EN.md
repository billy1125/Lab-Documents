# Read Before Joining: Undergraduate Project Teams

> Disclaimer: The following content may change over time or in response to events.

## 1. Let Me Be Clear First

The following are the basic ground rules.

### 🙅‍♂️ What I Will Not Do

- I will not ask any team member to handle any personal matters unrelated to the project
- I will not ask any team member to hold intensive meetings during final exam week or before important exams
- I will not use emotion or pressure as my main way of managing (for example, scolding or humiliation)
- I will not ask any member to work through social media outside working hours or on holidays
- I will not take credit for the team's project results or ignore any member's contribution
- I will not force the team to take part in activities unrelated to the project

### 🚫 Bottom Lines (Make Sure You Understand These)

#### Academic Bottom Line

If any of the following occurs, I will directly consider ending the advising relationship and handle it according to university regulations:

- **Any form of academic misconduct (copying, plagiarism, fabricating data)**
- **Using AI tools to generate core content without disclosing it, and presenting it as your own work**
- **Using other people's work or open-source code without citing the source**

> This is the basic threshold for producing any work, not a rule that only applies to research labs.

#### Performance Bottom Line

I will take the following seriously, and will first talk with you to confirm whether you are willing to improve:

- **No progress over a long period and no response to discussions**
- **Being out of contact for a long time, or missing meetings without notice in advance**
- **Clearly not cooperating with the team's division of work, and dumping work on other teammates**

> Every team member needs to take their own share of the work, and their teammates' time, seriously.

#### Conduct Bottom Line

The project team should maintain a collaborative environment based on mutual respect:

- No discrimination or harassment: any form of discrimination, bullying, or harassment is strictly prohibited
- No shifting of responsibility: shifting all of your own work onto others is not allowed for any reason
- Fair treatment: make sure every team member is treated fairly in the division of work

> If anything unreasonable happens, any member can tell me directly, or seek help through the relevant university channels.

## 2. How I Hope Undergraduate Projects Will Run

A project is not homework for a class. It is more like this:

- You first find a direction on your own, try methods, and adjust based on results
- I provide direction and suggestions, but I will not tell you how to do every step?
- There is no standard answer; what you build is the team's own result

What do I think undergraduate project students should have?

- Willingness to learn proactively, and to look for a solution yourself first when you hit a problem
- Not being afraid of uncertainty, and being willing to figure things out as you go
- Being able to divide work and cooperate with teammates, instead of waiting for others to finish everything

On the other hand, if what you are hoping for is:

- The professor gives clear steps and you just follow them
- There are seniors whose existing results you can copy directly
- You don't need to think much; you pass as long as you hand something in

I would suggest you talk with other professors first.

## 3. The Basic Team Culture I Want to Build

### 1. Take Responsibility

If a task is the team's responsibility, see it through to the end.

- You can ask when you are stuck, but you cannot skip trying altogether
- No one will keep reminding the team what to do
- If a problem is coming, raise it as early as possible and solve it together, instead of putting it off until the end

### 2. Work Done Must Be "Visible"

It does not count just because the team says it was done. It has to be verifiable, for example:

- There is code, there are records
- There are organized test results or data
- There are written records (weekly work log, progress updates)

### 3. Less Empty Talk, More Hands-On Work

Ideas matter, but what matters more is:

- Trying things quickly
- Doing small-scale validation
- Using results to adjust direction
- Writing it down

### 4. Uncertainty Is Normal

An unclear direction at the start and hitting one pitfall after another are expected.  
What matters is whether you keep moving forward and whether you build up meaningful attempts.

### 5. If the Team Feels Something Is Unreasonable

Say it directly. Most problems can be cleared up through communication.

### 6. Be Honest About Results

Whether results are good or bad, they need to be recorded and discussed truthfully:

- Results that do not meet expectations are normal, not a problem
- But you must not alter data or test conditions because you "want good-looking results"
- The parts you could not get working need to be explained just as clearly as the parts you did

## 4. Basic Team Rules

### 1. Regular Progress Updates

Before each meeting with me, every team member needs to prepare:

- What you did between the last meeting and this one
- What problems you ran into
- What you plan to do next

At the same time, please update the "**weekly work log**":

1. In the spring semester of your third year, we meet once or twice a month; please make one log entry per month
2. In the fall semester of your fourth year, there must be a discussion every week, and you must make two log entries per month

### 2. Before Discussing a Problem with Me

As far as possible, please first:

- Try at least 1–2 methods
- Be able to clearly explain the problem and where you are stuck

### 3. A Deadline Is a Commitment

- If the team has committed to anything, it is responsible for making every effort to deliver
- If you cannot deliver, explain in advance instead of stalling or disappearing

> Department deadlines in particular (submitting forms and reports) allow no exceptions. Make sure to finish early; do not leave it to the last day.

### 4. Communication Among Team Members

As a principle, I do not interfere in communication among team members, but I suggest that every member:

- Reply to messages within a reasonable time (non-urgent: within 24 hours)
- If you cannot attend because of something, let others know in advance instead of disappearing at the last minute
- If you disagree with a decision, say so directly within the team, instead of complaining privately or cooperating passively
- Keep basic courtesy among classmates, especially in interactions between people of different genders

### 6. AI Tool Use Principles

This project takes an open but cautious stance on generative AI (such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.):

- **Assist, not replace:** AI can be used to help with code, polish writing, or organize data, but it must not replace the team's thinking and core design
- **Responsibility for accuracy:** The team is fully responsible for AI-generated content, including any errors in it
- **Information security:** Uploading unpublished research data or confidential content to public AI platforms is prohibited
- **Transparent disclosure:** If AI assistance is used extensively, proactively state its scope and how it was used in the final report or results presentation

## 5. Project Goals

### Meeting the Department's Basic Requirements

First, understand the department's project rules, including all the requirements for the written report and the project presentations.

### Goals I Expect

- The basic project completion requirements set by the department
- Taking part in on-campus or external competitions or academic conferences

### Real Practice in Teamwork

- In your future workplace, it is hard to avoid working with others
- How to divide work, communicate with people, and handle conflict all need to be learned
- In class you can only learn knowledge about teamwork. Knowledge is not experience; only actually going through it becomes experience

### Experience You Can Talk About in Job Hunting or Entrance Exams

- "What I did, what problems I ran into, how I solved them"
- This is the most convincing thing you can offer when job hunting or in entrance exams

### My Thoughts

I hope you are not here just to finish an assignment to get it over with, but to:

- Really think through what you are doing and why you are doing it this way?
- Feel at least a little proud of what you build
- Learn things you can use in the future, because doing a project well helps you whether you go into a job or continue your studies

---

## 6. Finally: Every Team Member Should First Think About Three Questions

Before joining this project, please think about:

1. Can I accept the feeling of an unclear direction at the start, and having to figure things out myself?
2. Can I take on my own share of the work, without pushing the burden onto my teammates?
3. Am I willing to do this seriously, instead of just doing enough to pass?
4. I understand that doing a project is hard work

**Doing a project will be hard work. I hope that when you finish, you feel you have learned something and have results worth keeping.**

If everyone in the team agrees with all of the above, "all members must come together" to meet with me. Please note that coming for a meeting does not mean I have accepted you.

> The meeting is not a screening exam. It is a two-way check of whether the principles above match your expectations and plans.
